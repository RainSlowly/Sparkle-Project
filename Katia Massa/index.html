Connect student support services

Housing - Finance - Disability - Wellbeing - Immigration


Many students face situations where different difficulties are connected.
Housing issues, financial pressure, disability needs, wellbeing challenges, immigration situations, identity-related difficulties or personal crises can happen at the same time. However, the departments and offices that deal with these areas often operate separately and do not always communicate clearly with each other.
Because of this, students may find themselves moving between multiple offices, explaining their situation many times, trying to solve problems that are actually connected.
When someone is already struggling, this process can become confusing, stressful and very time-consuming. And sometimes the process doesn’t even bring students to the real office or institution that can actually help them. They can get stuck in the “in between”, being sent from one place to another, without a clear direction.

This is why having someone who can help you navigate makes a huge difference, not a psychologist or a lawyer, but a person who knows how the system works, who can guide you, direct you, and put you in contact with the right people.
Someone who helps untangle complicated situations and makes sure you reach the place that can actually help you.

In the UK there is already a similar concept called a social prescriber. This is a person who helps connect individuals with the right services and support when they are facing difficulties. They are not there to replace specialists, but to help people navigate the system and find the help they need.

In a similar way, I believe having someone who understands students’ realities and can help guide them toward the right support can make a big difference.

My goal is to improve coordination between housing, finance, disability wellbeing services so students do not feel alone navigating complicated systems in any kind of situation. By improving communication between offices and helping students reach the right place faster, it becomes easier to untangle situations and access the support that already exists.
In this way I want to build stronger bridges between students and institutions, so support systems become easier to navigate and students don’t feel invisible or left alone in the process

Give students a voice

Create spaces for student stories


I would like to give students more opportunities to express their experiences, ideas and concerns, especially around difficult topics that are not always easy to talk about.

One way to do this would be by organising discussions where students can share their perspectives and confront different points of view in a respectful environment.
If a topic is supported by many students and there is a clear need for dialogue, these discussions could also involve meetings with institutions and teachers so that important issues can be addressed together.

At the same time, I would like to give students the possibility to share their stories anonymously.

These experiences could then be communicated to the wider community through media and exhibitions, especially through visual arts such as film, video, theatre, music and painting. This would allow students who do not feel comfortable speaking publicly to still have their voices heard.
Through these artistic forms, it becomes possible to represent the many different difficulties people may encounter in life and during their studies, while also opening a space to reflect together on what kinds of support and help could make a difference in these situations.

Increase awareness of support

Help students discover tools available


Many students struggle simply because they are not aware of the tools and support already available. Sometimes rules, policies and support systems exist, but they can be difficult to find or not explained in a way that feels clear and accessible.

Students also come from many different countries and educational systems. In some places, structures like student representation or student unions do not really exist, or students are not used to having the possibility to influence institutions.
Because of this, some students may not realise the power they actually have to participate, share ideas, ask for support, or make their voices heard.
For this reason, I would like to improve communication so that the support already available becomes more visible and easier to understand.
One way to do this would be through clear explanations, accessible presentations and short videos that explain what students can do, what tools exist, and how they can access them that might be provided to all the student to inform them during the induction week.

These initiatives can also help give students a voice, showing that their experiences and perspectives matter and can contribute to improving the environment around them.
At the same time, raising awareness can help people better understand the different challenges students may face during their studies, including financial difficulties, wellbeing challenges, identity-related issues or experiences of discrimination or violence.
The goal is also to help normalise asking for help and reduce the stigma that sometimes surrounds these topics. When students understand the tools available to them and realise that their voices matter, they are much more likely to participate, support each other and contribute to positive change.

Together we can function as a human bridge for help and a safety net, supporting each other and helping connect people with the right resources , by sharing knowledge, and listening to experiences .

Raise awareness through art

Film – Theatre – Photography – Music


One of my projects will focus on raising awareness through art, such as short films, theatre, photography, painting and music.

My goal is to give students a space to raise their voices while enabling artists and creatives to transform these experiences into powerful visual stories that can create a meaningful impact on society.
In this way I can merge both my passion for helping others and my love for art, because art is a powerful tool to move people, raise awareness and create empathy — art is a tool to move souls.

These stories could be represented through exhibitions, short films, interviews, or theatre performances interpreted by actors, so that students can see real experiences without anyone feeling exposed or embarrassed, while still transforming personal experiences into voices that resonate and create understanding.

Through these creative initiatives I would also like to organise exhibitions, screenings and performances that raise funds to support students facing difficulties and charitable initiatives while also supporting emerging artists.

The Sparkle Project was born from a simple idea:
sometimes even a small spark can make a difference in someone’s life.

At university many students go through difficult moments : financial struggles, health issues, experiences of racism or exclusion, loneliness, sadness, or simply feeling lost. Often people feel they are alone in experiencing these things, or they feel they cannot speak freely.
They may be afraid of judgment, or they may not believe they can receive help. In some cases there is also distrust, or certain issues : such as disability, domestic violence or sexual violence ,
can be surrounded by stigma in some cultures and in society more generally. Because of this, it often becomes very difficult to talk about these experiences, and many people end up staying silent, or simply feel powerless, thinking that even if they speak nothing will change.

The goal of Sparkle is to create a space where students can share their experiences, anonymously if they wish, and realise that they are not alone. These can be personal stories of struggle, health, inclusion, discrimination, or simple moments of sadness or isolation.
Listening to each other can already be a powerful step toward understanding and support.

Through art, such as short films, theatre, photography, painting, music and exhibitions, these experiences can be transformed into powerful stories that raise awareness and empathy. Art has the power to move people in a way that statistics or policies sometimes cannot.
My goal is to give students a space to raise their voices while allowing artists and creatives to transform these experiences into meaningful visual stories that can create a real impact on society.
In this way, art becomes a powerful tool to move people, raise awareness and create empathy.

Through these creative initiatives, I would also like to organise exhibitions, screenings and performances that can raise funds to support students facing difficulties, as well as charitable initiatives, while also giving emerging artists a space to share their work.

Sparkle is about turning individual struggles into something that can create understanding, solidarity and positive change within the university community.

Through Sparkle, I am calling creatives to participate in this artistic project , including models, photographers, film directors, directors of photography , scriptwriters, musicians, actors and other artists. The idea is to bring together people who want to collaborate and use art to give voice to important experiences and topics.

Anyone who has a story to share, or who believes in the purpose of this project, is welcome to join and contribute to transforming these experiences into powerful artistic expressions.

A human bridge for Help

About me

My skills and experience As a former Student Representative and Deputy Lead Department Rep at UCL, I worked to improve communication between students and teachers, helping organise resources and schedules so everyone could carry out their work more effectively. In this role I learned to listen to others, represent their needs and communicate them clearly in order to find practical compromises within the limits of time and organisation. During this experience I also worked to address a gap in our course when an important subject for many careers in our field was cancelled. I advocated for alternative ways for students to gain that knowledge, and in the following years the subject was eventually introduced as part of the curriculum. I have also been involved in charity initiatives supporting Cystic Fibrosis and have experience organising events and advertising campaigns in London for companies working across different cultures and communities. I have always used art as a way to express myself first through painting, and later through singing and theatre. At one point I met a film director who showed me another way to express these ideas. It made me realise that we all need to find our own way to speak up and make a difference. These experiences strengthened my leadership, organisational and communication skills, but most importantly they reinforced my belief that our voices matter and that by working together we can contribute to positive change.
